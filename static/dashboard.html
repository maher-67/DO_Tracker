Expected units per device over the chosen period. These are rough estimates: orders arrive in lumps, so they can miss by a wide margin. To decide what to order, use Key Metrics → PAR and ordering position.

How is this calculated?
Projected demand = Trend × Seasonal factor for each month, then added up
  • Trend – a straight line through the monthly sales history. Is demand rising or falling?
  • Seasonal factor – how much busier or quieter a calendar month usually is. 1.20 means 20% busier than normal.
Example. The trend says 500 units for March, and March is usually 20% busier (1.20). Projected demand for March = 500 × 1.20 = 600 units. Add up the months in the chosen period for the total.

What counts as “confirmed”?

A delivery is counted when all three are true:

  1. It is an outgoing delivery from the warehouse to a customer.
  2. Its status is Waiting or Ready.
  3. Its Ops Status is Shipment in Progress.
Units are the quantity ordered on each line, for tracked devices only. Accessories on the same delivery are not counted.

How is revenue calculated?
Annual revenue = Devices delivered × Annual fee per device for each device, then added up
  • Devices delivered – units on lines going to a customer location, from transfers that are Done.
  • Added products – products you add under the fee table count as devices too.
  • Spares – left out when “Don't count spares” is ticked. They come from the file's Spare Quantity column.
  • Skipped – internal moves and returns are not deliveries to customers. They appear on the Warehouse Shipments tab.
  • The file needs a product column (SKU or Internal Reference) and a quantity column.
Example. A device with a $120 annual fee and 50 units delivered adds 50 × $120 = $6,000 a year.

What counts as “shipped out”?
Shipped out = Units on Done transfers from the warehouse − Moves that stay inside the warehouse
  • Counted – units that went to customers, to vendors (returns) or to other company locations such as HO/Stock.
  • Not counted – moves inside the warehouse, such as to Pre-Production. They are listed for reference.
  • Skipped – lines shipped from another location (for example HO/Stock). They did not leave this warehouse.
  • Spares – included, because they physically leave.
Example. 40 devices moved from WH/Stock to HO/Stock count as shipped out. 40 devices moved from WH/Stock to WH/Stock/Pre-Production do not.

Warehouse turns

How many times our average stock was sold and replaced over the last 12 months. Higher means stock moves faster.

How is this calculated?
Turns = Units shipped in the last 12 months ÷ Average stock on hand
Days of cover = Stock on hand now ÷ Units shipped per day (units shipped ÷ 365)
  • Units shipped – delivered to customers over the last 12 months.
  • Average stock on hand – the average of 13 snapshots: today, plus the same day of each of the last 12 months.
  • Days of cover – how long today's stock would last at the usual pace.
  • Always covers the last 12 months. The time period buttons above do not change it.

PAR and ordering position

How much stock we should hold, what we effectively have, and how much to order now.

How is this calculated?
PAR = Average daily shipments × Lead time (days) × 1 + Safety stock
Position = On hand now − Outgoing confirmed + Buildable + Buildable from incoming parts
Order now = PAR − Position never below 0, rounded to the nearest 100
Buildable = Smallest of (parts on hand ÷ parts needed per unit) across all of a device's parts
  • PAR – the stock level we want to hold: enough to cover shipments while a new order arrives, plus a safety buffer. Rounded to the nearest 100. If Odoo has a PAR for a device, that number is used instead.
  • Lead time – the Delivery Lead Time on the product's Purchase tab in Odoo. With no vendor in Odoo, the default in the box is used.
  • Safety stock – an extra buffer on top of expected demand. It starts at the default above.
  • Average daily shipments – units shipped to customers in the last 12 months ÷ 365.
  • Outgoing confirmed – units already promised to customers. They are about to leave.
  • Buildable – units we could assemble today from parts on hand. The scarcest part sets the limit.
  • Buildable from incoming parts – extra units we could assemble once parts already on order arrive.
  • Shared parts – when several devices use the same part, it is split between them in proportion to how much each one ships.
  • Devices we buy finished, like HEX-G, have no parts, so only stock on hand counts for them.
  • Sliders – each device has its own lead time and safety stock slider. A blue slider means you changed it. ↺ puts it back.
  • Tip – hover a number to see which part is the limit, or click a device's Incoming parts cell for a part-by-part breakdown.

PAR by vendor

Each device is listed under the vendor that sets its lead time: its own vendor, or for a device we build, the vendor of its slowest part.

How are vendors chosen?
  1. A device we build takes its lead time from its slowest part. That part's vendor is the one shown.
  2. A device we buy finished takes it from the device's own vendor.
  3. A product with several vendors follows the choice above the table: the first listed in Odoo, the slowest, or the fastest.
  4. Moving a device's lead time slider changes its PAR but not its vendor. The device is marked “adjusted”.
  5. With no vendor lead time in Odoo, the default lead time is used and the device is grouped under “No vendor lead time in Odoo”.

To change a vendor or a lead time, edit it on the product's Purchase tab in Odoo. The dashboard picks it up on the next refresh.

Delivery cycle time

How many days a delivery takes, from the day it is Waiting to the day it is Done.

How is this calculated?
Cycle time (days) = Date marked Done − Date it became Waiting
  • Average – all the cycle times added up, divided by the number of deliveries.
  • Median – the middle value: half of the deliveries were faster, half were slower.
  • Unusually long deliveries are left out of both, so one stuck order does not distort them. The page says how many.
  • All deliveries counts every completed delivery to a customer. Device-only counts deliveries where every line is one of our tracked devices.
Example. Waiting on October 1 and Done on October 5 is a cycle time of 4 days.

Sales order age at fulfillment

Count by:

How long customers waited from placing an order to shipment, grouped by age.

How is this calculated?
Order age (days) = Ship date − Sales order date
  • Each delivery goes into a group: 0–90, 90–180, 180–360 or 360+ days. Exactly 90 days is in the 90–180 group.
  • Devices counts the tracked devices on those deliveries. Orders counts the deliveries themselves.
  • Only deliveries linked to a sales order are included, and accessories are not counted.
  • It includes the time before a delivery even starts processing, so it is longer than the cycle time above.
Example. Ordered on January 10 and shipped on June 20 is 161 days, so it falls in the “90–180 days” group.
